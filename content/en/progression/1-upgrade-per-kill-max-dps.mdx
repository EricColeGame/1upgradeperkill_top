### Picking a Class for the Lobby You're In

- **Crowded free-for-all:** Bomber and Firestarter. Area damage lets you steal kills and finish low-health players, which is the fastest route to max DPS in messy lobbies.
- **Open, spread-out maps:** Archer and Skirmisher. You want poke and mobility rather than commitment.
- **Duels and small fights:** Warrior and Scorcher. Straight-line damage wins trades when nobody can third-party.
- **Learning the ladder:** Trickster or Trapper. Both reward game sense over raw mechanics, so you can practice positioning without dying every ten seconds.

Player experience suggests that class choice matters less than discipline. A patient Archer at Iron usually out-earns an impatient Warrior at Copper, because the Archer keeps their streak alive long enough to actually climb.

The Gear Saver is the single most underrated item in the game. Community reports consistently place it at the top of the priority list for anyone pushing leaderboards.

Enchant rolling is a gamble, so set a budget. Roll for damage first, mobility second, and stop once you have a combination that wins trades at your current tier — chasing a perfect roll usually costs more Gold than it returns.

## Rotation and Target Priority: Converting Damage Into Streaks

Max DPS is not just a number; it is a decision tree. The arena uses crits, knockback, and real armor values, so the player who picks the right target wins more fights than the player with slightly better gear.

| Target Type | When to Take It | Risk Level |
|---|---|---|
| Low-tier player (Leather/Copper) | Whenever they are alone | Low — best bonus XP value |
| Isolated mid-tier (Iron/Diamond) | When you have armor advantage | Medium — high upgrade payoff |
| High-upgrade player | Only with a health or numbers advantage | High — worth bonus XP, but costly to lose |
| Group of two or more | Almost never, unless you have area damage | Very high — third parties love groups |
| Retreating player | Yes, but only briefly | Medium — over-chasing gets you killed |

Practical habits that raise your effective DPS:

- **Open with your strongest ability, not your weakest.** Burst first, then clean up — the faster a target drops, the less time their teammates have to react.
- **Use knockback to break healing and reset spacing.** It is a damage tool disguised as crowd control.
- **Fight near cover, not in the open.** Real armor values reward short trades, and cover lets you disengage before your streak is at risk.
- **Leave fights you are not winning.** Every death resets progress, so a retreat at 30% health is often a DPS gain over five seconds of pride.

## FAQ

**What is the fastest way to reach 1 Upgrade Per Kill max DPS?**

Pick a class that matches your lobby, roll damage enchants early, and farm isolated low-tier players for bonus XP. Most players climb fastest by avoiding crowded fights until they hit Iron or Diamond, then using the armor advantage to win every trade.

**Does dying really reset all my upgrades?**

Yes. Death sends your gear back to zero, which is why Gear Savers matter so much at high tiers. Using one at Shadow or Prism preserves armor you would otherwise have to earn again from Leather.

**Which class has the highest damage in 1 Upgrade Per Kill?**

It depends on the fight. Bomber and Firestarter dominate crowded arenas through area damage, while Warrior and Scorcher win straight duels. Archer and Skirmisher offer safer, more consistent output if your positioning is strong.

**Is chasing high-upgrade players worth it for max DPS?**

Sometimes. Hunting high-upgrade targets grants bonus XP, but they usually have better armor and more practice. Take those fights only with a health advantage, numbers, or a Gear Saver in reserve — otherwise you are feeding someone else's streak.
