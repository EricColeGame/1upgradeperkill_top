### Which class should you actually pick?

For the **1 Upgrade Per Kill fastest way to upgrade** on a fresh account, ranged and disengage-friendly classes are the lowest-variance choice. Archer lets you chip from safety, while Skirmisher and Trickster give you an out when a fight turns. No. Class changes reset your feel for spacing, cooldowns, and engage range. If you're chasing a Best Streak record, lock in one class and learn its limits before you start climbing. Save experimentation for sessions where you're already planning to reset.

## A Phase-by-Phase Route From Leather to MAXED

Knowing the tiers is one thing; sequencing them is another. The route below splits a climb into four phases, each with a different objective. This is the practical version of the **1 Upgrade Per Kill fastest way to upgrade** — you're not playing the same way at Leather that you play at Prism.

| Phase | Your tier | Main goal | Best moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | Leather → Iron | Survive the first three kills | Fight at the edges, finish weakened players, disengage early |
| Mid-ladder | Iron → Full Diamond | Convert safety into volume | Rotate between fights, avoid center-stage duels |
| Upper ladder | Diamond → Shadow | Protect the streak | Gear Saver up, stick to group fights, refuse 1v1 ego duels |
| Endgame | Prism → MAXED | Don't lose it | Slow down, take only clear wins, let others come to you |

**The opening phase is where most runs die.** In Leather you have no armor buffer, so a fair fight is a coin flip you can't afford. Play the perimeter of the arena, watch for players already low after a duel, and take the free upgrade. Three safe kills get you to Iron, where the game finally starts rewarding patience.

**The mid-ladder is about volume.** At Iron and Diamond you can absorb a mistake or two, which means you can take more fights per minute. This is the window to build the streak that carries you to Shadow — and the window where a Gear Saver pays for itself.

**The endgame is a different game.** At Prism, everyone in the lobby can see what you're worth, and the XP bounty on high-Upgrade players makes you a magnet. Slow your pace, hug cover, and let the mid-tier players fight each other while you pick the survivor.

## FAQ

**What is the 1 Upgrade Per Kill fastest way to upgrade?**
Play for survival first, kills second. Pick a ranged or disengage-friendly class, fight only at the edges of the arena, third-party other players' duels instead of starting your own, and protect anything above Full Diamond with a Gear Saver. Consistent safe kills beat highlight-reel aggression every time.

**Do you really lose all progress when you die?**
Yes — a death sends your gear back to zero, which is the core tension of the mode. The Gear Saver exists specifically to soften that penalty at high Upgrade tiers, so it's worth prioritizing over almost any other purchase.

**Which class is best for a beginner?**
Archer is the friendliest entry point because ranged pressure lets you contribute to fights without standing in them. If you prefer melee, Warrior is straightforward but punishes positioning errors far harder.

**How long does it take to reach MAXED gear?**
That depends entirely on how often you die, not how often you kill. A player who averages a long, careful streak will reach Prism and MAXED far faster than someone with a higher kill count and a habit of trading deaths — which is exactly why the survival-first approach is the fastest route up the ladder.
