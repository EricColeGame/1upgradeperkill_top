## Why Damage Scaling Decides Every Fight in 1 Upgrade Per Kill

One kill, one upgrade — that's the whole promise of the game, and it's also why a single lost fight can snowball into a lost lobby. If your damage isn't climbing faster than your opponents' armor, you stop being able to finish kills, and once you stop finishing kills, you stop upgrading. This 1 Upgrade Per Kill damage tutorial is built around that loop: how eliminations convert into raw power, which classes get the most value out of every takedown, and which habits keep your stack growing instead of resetting.

Whether you're brand new or chasing a Halloween class, this 1 Upgrade Per Kill damage tutorial answers one question: how do you turn each kill into more damage than the last?

Here's the core idea in plain terms:

- **Damage compounds.** Every elimination raises your upgrade count, so a three-kill lead is a power lead, not just a score lead.
- **Armor punishes slow starts.** High-upgrade players are consistently described by the community as "armored" targets — they take far more hits to bring down.
- **Dying is expensive.** Losing your stack means re-entering fights at a disadvantage against players who never stopped farming.
- **Kits decide conversion.** A class perk like extra Golden Apples or a stack of TNT is what turns an upgrade advantage into actual kills.

## How Kills Convert Into Damage

Before you touch a class menu, understand what a kill actually pays you. In 1 Upgrade Per Kill, a takedown feeds two separate tracks at once — your upgrade count and your class level — and both matter for damage output.

| Action | What you gain | Why it matters for damage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eliminating a player | +1 upgrade (the game's core rule) | Raises your power tier so hits land harder against armored targets |
| Kills toward class level | Progress on that class's own level track | Unlocks stronger kit perks at each level |
| Knocking a player into the void | A kill regardless of their armor | Bypasses high-upgrade defense entirely |
| Surviving a fight | Keeps your existing upgrade total | Protects the snowball you already built |
| Dying | Loses your stack | Forces you to re-farm from a weaker position |

## The 1 Upgrade Per Kill Damage Tutorial, Step by Step

This is the practical sequence. Follow it in order and your damage curve should look like a staircase instead of a roller coaster.

| Phase | Your focus | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Kit selection | Pick a class you can win isolated trades with | Buying an expensive class before you can fight |
| 2. First kills | Farm isolated players away from groups | Fair fights in the open at zero upgrades |
| 3. Class progress | Stack kills toward your next class level | Splitting kills across too many classes |
| 4. Snowball | Take void knock-offs and third-party fights | Chasing runners into crowded areas |
| 5. Protection | Use escape perks to preserve your stack | Overextending for one extra kill |

**Step 1 — Choose a kit, not a fantasy.** Warrior is free and forgiving. Firestarter and Bomber are free during the Halloween event through the Halloween tab in Quests, though the exact quest steps aren't confirmed yet. Don't spend Gold on a class you can't use yet.

**Step 2 — Farm the first three kills on isolated targets.** Your early game is about volume, not highlights. Every isolated player you eliminate is a permanent upgrade; every 50/50 duel in a crowd is a coin flip on your entire stack.

**Step 3 — Let class levels ride along.** Warrior needs 50 kills plus 750 Gold to reach Level II, and Firestarter and Bomber level purely from kills. Because upgrade and class progress share the same trigger, efficient farming improves both at once.

**Step 4 — Convert the lead with void kills.** Once you're ahead, stop taking even fights. Snowballs, TNT, and cobweb pins all create eliminations that don't care about your target's armor — the single most reliable way to break a rival's snowball.

**Step 5 — Protect the stack.** A high upgrade count is only valuable if you keep it. Escape perks exist for exactly this: disengage, reset, re-engage on your terms.

## Upgrade Priorities and the Damage Mistakes That Stall You

Most players don't lose because of aim. They lose because of sequencing. Here are the recurring problems, based on community reports and class descriptions.

| Mistake | Why it hurts your damage | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taking even fights at 0 upgrades | You have no armor and no damage advantage | Farm isolated players first |
| Spreading kills across classes | Neither class level advances quickly | Commit to one class per session |
| Ignoring void knock-offs | You skip the fastest way to kill armored players | Learn edge positioning |
| Buying a class you can't level | Locked classes can't gain kills until purchased | Unlock, then commit |
| Overextending while ahead | One death erases your entire stack | Use escape perks to reset |

A simple priority order works for almost every run:

1. **Survive the opening.** Don't be the first player eliminated.
2. **Farm isolated targets.** Volume beats highlight-reel plays early.
3. **Hit your first class level.** The perk usually changes how you fight.
4. **Take only favorable fights.** Third-party fights are free upgrades.
5. **Protect the lead.** Escape tools are damage tools when they save your stack.

## FAQ

**What's the fastest way to raise damage in 1 Upgrade Per Kill?**
Chain kills on isolated players so your upgrade count never stalls, then use void knock-offs against armored opponents. A 1 Upgrade Per Kill damage tutorial can teach the theory, but the execution is just consistent, low-risk eliminations.

**Do void kills count as eliminations?**
Community reports say yes — knocking a player off the map credits the kill and the upgrade. Treat that as player experience rather than a confirmed rule until you've tested it in your own lobby.

**Is Warrior good enough for a damage-focused run?**
Absolutely. Warrior is free, comes with extra Golden Apples, and reaches Level II with 50 kills plus 750 Gold, starting you with 2 apples and a max of 5. It's the safest class to learn the upgrade loop on.

**Are Firestarter and Bomber really free?**
During the Halloween event, both are listed as "Free for a Halloween quest" in the Halloween tab of Quests. Outside the event, Firestarter costs 2,000 Gold and Bomber costs 4,000 Gold.

The takeaway from this 1 Upgrade Per Kill damage tutorial is simple: damage isn't something you find, it's something you accumulate. Protect your stack, farm efficiently, and let every kill pay for the next one.
