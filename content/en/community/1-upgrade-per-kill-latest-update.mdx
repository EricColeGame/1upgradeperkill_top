## The Upgrade Ladder: Eight Steps From Leather to MAXED

The ladder is the whole game. The page lists the progression as Leather → Copper → Iron → Full Diamond → Ruby → Shadow → Prism → MAXED. That is eight discrete steps, which means you need seven consecutive kills just to reach the top tier — and one death to lose all of it.

| Tier | Step | What it represents in practice |
|---|---|---|
| Leather | 0 kills | Default spawn gear; expect to lose most duels |
| Copper | 1 kill | Slight buffer; still fragile against anyone above Iron |
| Iron | 2 kills | First tier where you can trade hits and survive |
| Full Diamond | 3 kills | The "real armor" milestone most players aim for |
| Ruby | 4 kills | Snowball territory; you start winning by default |
| Shadow | 5 kills | High-value target — everyone wants your streak |
| Prism | 6 kills | One fight away from the ceiling |
| MAXED | 7+ kills | Fully upgraded; you become the lobby's boss fight |

The design is elegant because it inverts normal PvP incentives. In most arena games, the strongest player wants to fight constantly. Here, the strongest player wants to fight *carefully*, because the risk-reward curve flips the moment you pass Full Diamond.

Player experience suggests the practical sweet spot is Diamond through Ruby. At those tiers you can win most matchups against lower-geared opponents, but you are not yet glowing like a beacon for every player hunting bonus XP. Pushing past Shadow is where streaks usually die — not because the gear is worse, but because the whole lobby starts coordinating against you.

If you are brand new, start with Warrior or Skirmisher. They teach you spacing and knockback timing without asking you to manage traps or burn stacks at the same time.

The Gear Saver is the single most misunderstood item in the game. New players hoard them and never use them; experienced players burn them at exactly the right moment. The general rule from community reports: do not spend a Gear Saver below Diamond, and do not save one "for later" when you are sitting on a Prism streak in a lobby full of Archers. Insurance exists to be used.

Gold and perks are where long-term progress lives. Because your armor resets on death, Gold is the one thing that keeps accumulating no matter how badly a session goes. That makes perk purchases the closest thing this game has to a permanent power curve.

## Leaderboards and the Art of Hunting High-Upgrade Players

Three leaderboards are listed on the game page: Best Streak, Top Kills, and Fastest Diamond. They reward three completely different playstyles, which is a smarter design than a single kill counter.

| Leaderboard | What it measures | How to climb it |
|---|---|---|
| Best Streak | Longest run without dying | Play passively above Shadow tier |
| Top Kills | Lifetime or seasonal kill volume | Fight constantly at low tiers |
| Fastest Diamond | Speed from spawn to Full Diamond | Spawn-camp high-traffic areas early |

The Fastest Diamond board is the most underrated. It rewards raw aggression in the first three kills, which is exactly when you have the least to lose. If you are chasing it, do not bother upgrading past Diamond — reset and go again.

There is also the bounty system: hunting high-upgrade players grants bonus XP. That mechanic is why Prism and MAXED players cannot relax. The moment you hit the top of the ladder, you stop being a player and start being a public event. Smart high-tier players rotate to the edges of the arena and avoid open sightlines, while smart low-tier players patrol the center waiting for someone shiny to walk past.
