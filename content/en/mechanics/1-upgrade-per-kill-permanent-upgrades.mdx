## The Kill-to-Upgrade Ladder: How to Stack Advantages Fast

Because each elimination is worth exactly one upgrade, the fastest way to snowball is to take fights you can finish quickly and avoid fights that drag. A long, even trade costs you tempo even when you win it — you burn health, you burn position, and you announce your location to everyone nearby.

Class choice changes how you convert that tempo. The roster leans on three recognizable roles, and each one converts a kill into the next kill differently.

| Class | Range profile | Best at | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warrior | Close-range pressure | Finishing weakened targets fast | Crossing open ground under fire |
| Archer | Ranged poke | Softening targets and stealing third-party kills | Getting closed on while repositioning |
| Trapper | Area denial | Locking chokepoints and forcing bad fights | Setup time before a push arrives |

A few habits that consistently convert into faster stacks:

- **Take the first clean fight, not the first fight.** Early eliminations are the cheapest upgrades you'll ever get, but only if the trade is in your favor.
- **Rotate after every kill.** Your new gear step is only worth something if you survive long enough to use it.
- **Play the bridge.** Bridges and narrow crossings are natural chokepoints — the map art for the game literally centers on armored fighters meeting across one, and that's not an accident of design.
- **Respect the third party.** The loudest fight in the lobby attracts the healthiest player. Clean up, then move.
- **Learn one class deeply.** Knowing exactly how far a Warrior can close before you commit is worth more than a single extra gear step.

Community reports also suggest that stacking upgrades in the *same* life is where the real power spike lives. Two upgrades in one life feels like a small edge; five in one life feels like a different game entirely. That compounding is the entire point of a per-kill system, and it's why surviving matters more than any individual duel.

## How to Protect Your Stack and Recover After a Reset

Dying is not a failure state in +1 Upgrade Per Kill — it's the loop's reset condition, and it will happen. The skill is in minimizing how often it happens at a bad moment and in restarting efficiently when it does.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Better habit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chasing a runner with a fresh upgrade | You trade your stack for one more step | Let them leave; re-enter the fight on your terms |
| Fighting in the open after a kill | Your position is already known | Break line of sight, reset, then re-engage |
| Playing a new class every session | Class reads never sharpen | Pick a main and a backup |
| Ignoring the code menu | Free gifts sit unclaimed | Check codes once a week |
| Re-entering the same fight on respawn | Predictable pathing gets punished | Take a different lane back into the action |

Recovery is a mindset more than a mechanic. The moment you respawn, you're back at the bottom of the ladder with a full health bar and a lobby full of players who are mid-climb. That's actually an advantage: they're the ones with something to lose. Play greedy for the first two kills, then tighten up once you're the one holding a stack.

## FAQ

### Are 1 Upgrade Per Kill permanent upgrades actually permanent?

The in-match gear ladder is not — the game's progression is built around a climb that resets on death, according to community reports. What behaves like permanent progress is the account-level layer: redeemed code gifts, one-time group rewards, and your own class knowledge.

### How many kills do I need for a meaningful power spike?

Each elimination is worth exactly one upgrade, so the ladder is linear by design. The important number isn't total kills — it's kills *within a single life*, because those stack without interruption. Two or three clean eliminations in one run is where most players start to feel the snowball.

### Which class should I main if I want to stack fast?

Warriors convert kills quickest because close-range fights end faster, but they also die fastest. Archers are safer for players still learning positioning, and Trappers reward players who like controlling where fights happen. Pick whichever one you can survive longest with — survival is what protects your stack.

### Do codes reset when I die?

No. Code rewards and group gifts sit outside the kill-based gear ladder entirely, which is exactly why they're the most reliable form of 1 Upgrade Per Kill permanent upgrades available right now. Just remember that most codes are one claim per account and can expire.

## The Bottom Line

Treat +1 Upgrade Per Kill as two games running at once: a fast, risky gear sprint inside each life, and a slow, safe account grind outside it. Win the sprint by taking cheap fights early and rotating hard after every kill. Win the grind by checking codes, joining the community group, and refusing to switch classes every session. Players who separate those two layers stop feeling punished by the reset — and start using it as a reason to play the next run a little sharper than the last.
