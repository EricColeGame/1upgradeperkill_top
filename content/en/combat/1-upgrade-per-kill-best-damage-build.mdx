## How Damage Scaling Actually Works in 1 Upgrade Per Kill

The core loop is a snowball. You kill, you gain an upgrade, you get stronger, and you kill faster. The reverse is also true: one bad fight can erase everything, because your Upgrade resets to 0 on death unless you're holding a Gear Saver.

That makes "damage" and "survival" the same stat in practice. A player with 12 upgrades and no health buffer is worth less than a player with 6 upgrades who never dies. Community reports and gameplay footage from the game's first month both point to the same pattern — the players at the top of a 25-player server are the ones who protect their stack, not the ones who take every fight.

With more than 2.1 million visits recorded by late October 2026, lobbies fill quickly, so you rarely get a quiet stretch to rebuild. Plan for constant contact.

## The 1 Upgrade Per Kill Best Damage Build, Step by Step

There is no officially published tier list yet, so treat any specific numbers you see online with suspicion. What follows is a priority framework based on how the upgrade economy works and on player experience in public lobbies — not a datamined spreadsheet.

| Rank | Upgrade category | Priority | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Damage per hit | Must-have | Buy this first after every respawn |
| 2 | Attack speed / swing rate | High | Multiplies your existing damage for free |
| 3 | Max health / extra hearts | High | Protects the stack you just built |
| 4 | Mobility and dash options | Medium | Escape tool more than a kill tool |
| 5 | Economy and utility | Situational | Only worth it once your stack is safe |

**The opening sequence.** After your first kill, put the point into damage. Not health, not movement, not a novelty option — damage. The faster your second kill arrives, the faster the whole build compounds.

**The mid-run pivot.** Once you're stringing kills together, layer in attack speed. Two damage upgrades with faster swings will outperform three damage upgrades at base speed in most fights, because the game rewards finishing a target before they can heal with a Golden Apple.

**The insurance point.** Before you push into a crowded area, spend one upgrade on survivability. This is the point most players skip, and it's the reason their 15-stack run ends at 4.

**The reset plan.** After a death, ignore bounties and quests for two minutes. Farm the safest nearby target, rebuild your first two damage upgrades, and only then rejoin the main fight. Chasing a bounty with zero upgrades is how a bad minute becomes a bad session.

## Best Classes for a Damage Build

The community hasn't settled on a tested class ranking yet, but the three known classes push you toward very different playstyles. If your goal is the 1 Upgrade Per Kill best damage build, the choice is mostly about how forgiving the class is while you learn the map.

| Class | Damage-build fit | Strengths | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warrior | Best for learning | Extra Golden Apples per life, forgiving health economy | Fewer escape tools in open ground |
| Trickster | Situational | Can knock players into the void for instant kills | Kills from knockback don't teach you damage trading |
| Bomber | Situational | Area denial that punishes crowded fights | Chaotic in tight spaces with teammates nearby |

**Why Warrior wins for most players.** Extra apples mean extra mistakes allowed. A damage build lives or dies on how many hits you can absorb between heals, and Warrior simply gives you more room to learn the timing of a fight without losing your stack.

**When Trickster or Bomber makes sense.** If you're already confident in your movement, void kills are the cheapest eliminations in the game — no damage race required. Just understand that you're playing a positioning game, not a damage game, and your upgrade purchases should reflect that.

## Mistakes That Kill a Damage Build

Most failed runs aren't lost to better opponents. They're lost to small decisions that compound.

- **Buying utility before damage.** Economy upgrades pay off later; damage upgrades pay off now. In a game where your stack resets, "later" is a gamble.
- **Fighting near edges.** Trickster and Bomber exist to turn positioning errors into instant deaths. Fight with your back to solid ground.
- **Healing too late.** Golden Apples aren't a panic button. Eat at half health, not at a sliver.
- **Chasing bounties on a fresh respawn.** You're the weakest player in the lobby for the first 30 seconds. Play like it.
- **Ignoring the Gear Saver.** If your goal is a deep stack, this is the single highest-value purchase in the game.
- **Skipping the health buffer.** One extra heart often decides whether a 20-stack run becomes a 3-stack run.

| Bad habit | Immediate cost | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| All-in on damage | Instant death to one burst | Buy one health upgrade per 4 kills |
| Edge fighting | Void deaths | Rotate toward map center |
| Hoarding gold | Slow scaling | Spend on upgrades every life |
| Ignoring quests | Missed passive income | Check the quest tab each session |

## FAQ

**What is the 1 Upgrade Per Kill best damage build for beginners?**
Start with Warrior, buy damage on your first kill, add attack speed next, and pick up one survivability upgrade before you push into crowded areas. Keep a Gear Saver if you can, and always carry at least two Golden Apples into a fight.

**Do my damage upgrades reset when I die?**
Yes — your Upgrade resets to 0 on death unless you're using a Gear Saver. That's why the 1 Upgrade Per Kill best damage build treats survival upgrades as part of the damage plan rather than a separate defensive build.

**Which class should I pick for a pure damage playstyle?**
Warrior is the most forgiving choice and the easiest class to learn the damage loop on. Trickster and Bomber can rack up kills through void knockbacks, but those eliminations reward positioning rather than raw damage output.
