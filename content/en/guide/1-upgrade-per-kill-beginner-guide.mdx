## How the Upgrade Loop Actually Works

Think of your upgrade count as a snowball sitting on a slope. Every kill makes it bigger, and every death throws it back to the bottom. Players who treat each fight as a fair 1v1 lose their stack constantly; players who treat each fight as a risk assessment keep it.

| Situation | What happens | How to protect yourself |
|---|---|---|
| You eliminate a player | Your upgrade count goes up by one | Chain fights while you're ahead |
| You die | Upgrade count resets to zero | Carry a Gear Saver if you can |
| You take heavy damage | You're one hit from losing everything | Disengage and heal with Golden Apples |
| You're low on health and out of apples | You're gambling your whole stack | Leave the fight, reset, come back |

A few practical takeaways from that table:

- **Play the clock, not the ego.** When you're up several upgrades, you're the richest target in the lobby. Every player nearby wants what you have.
- **Heal before you're empty.** Golden Apples are your reset button. Warrior players get extra apples, and the Snack Pack perk reportedly adds one more per life — both are strong picks for anyone who expects to take damage.
- **Treat Gear Savers as insurance, not a strategy.** Community reports describe them as a way to keep your upgrades after a death, but leaning on them teaches bad habits. Learn to survive first.

The most common mistake in this game isn't mechanical — it's continuing a fight you've already lost because the upgrade is "worth it." It never is. The stack is worth more than the kill.

## Best Classes for New Players

There's no officially tested tier list yet, and anyone claiming otherwise is guessing. What community reports do agree on is that Warrior is the friendliest starting class because the extra Golden Apples give you room to make mistakes, while Trickster and Bomber can shove opponents into the void for instant eliminations.

| Class | Main strength | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warrior | Extra Golden Apples, forgiving sustain | Your first several matches | Low burst — you win long fights, not quick ones |
| Trickster | Can knock players into the void | Flanking and punishing edge-huggers | Requires positioning awareness |
| Bomber | Can knock players into the void | Area control and crowded fights | Easy to whiff if you rush |
| Others | Not yet ranked by the community | Experimenting | No reliable data yet |

If you're brand new, spend your first session on Warrior. You'll lose fewer upgrades to chip damage, and you'll learn the map without dying to every stray hit. Once you can reliably get two or three kills in a row, switch to Trickster or Bomber and start hunting players near ledges — the void is the great equalizer, and it doesn't care how many upgrades your target has.

Mobile players shouldn't feel locked out either. Gameplay footage used for community documentation was recorded on mobile, so touch controls are clearly viable. Expect a slightly slower reaction time and lean harder on positioning to compensate.
